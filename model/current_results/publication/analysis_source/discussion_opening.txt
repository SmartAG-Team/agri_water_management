We used a calibrated crop model to simulate continuous winter wheat–summer maize rotations across the North China Plain, compare uniform and spatially targeted irrigation under equal water budgets, and test whether antecedent regional water storage improved decisions based on rainfall. Sustaining grain production under restricted irrigation requires consideration of both consumptive water use and the distribution of production losses across locations. The conditional results show that higher regional grain retention can accompany greater evapotranspiration and lower production in some locations, whereas storage information did not change the selected irrigation quotas within the tested design. The management value of these strategies therefore depends on accounting for the complete rotation water balance, soil-water carry-over across crops and fallows, and reliable crop responses to irrigation.
